Computer you already have is enough if it can run a browser and a slicer. Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer run on a normal Windows, Mac, or Linux laptop from the last several years. Buy a computer only if yours cannot run the slicer. Do not buy a workstation.

CAD software now: install **FreeCAD** (free, offline, commercial use is permitted under its license; confirm the current license text) or **Onshape** only on a paid plan. Onshape's free plan publishes documents. Do not design sellable parts in a public document. Autodesk's free Fusion personal license has commercial limits that change; if you use Fusion, read the current license and pay for a commercial seat once you are selling. Budget $0 until the first sale, then about $70 a month if you choose paid Fusion. **RECOMMENDATION:** start in FreeCAD or a paid Onshape trial. Hire a designer before you subscribe for a year.

Slicer: **Bambu Studio** if you buy a Bambu, or **OrcaSlicer**. Both are free. Do not pay for a slicer.

## Accounts and paper, in the order you need them

**Now, free.** Google account for the sheet. eBay account if you do not have one. You can list later. Amazon account waits until a prototype fits.

**Before the first paid order ships.**

1. Form an LLC in your state through the secretary of state's website. Filing fees vary by state from tens of dollars to a few hundred. Look up your state. Do not use a $300 "formation service" unless you want the convenience; the state filing is the actual requirement.
2. Get an EIN from irs.gov. It is free. The online assistant issues it immediately for most domestic applicants.
3. Open a business checking account in the LLC's name and a card for filament and postage. Amazon will deposit to a bank account. Use the business account, not your personal spending account.
4. A spreadsheet with columns: date, SKU, units, revenue, fees, postage, material grams, print minutes, refunds. That is the bookkeeping system until 50 orders.
5. Insurance quote for general liability and product liability. Tell the broker you sell small 3D-printed replacement parts and name the categories you refused (no children's, no food contact, no auto safety). Bind before you ship a functional part.

**When the prototype fits and you are ready to list on Amazon.**

Go to sellercentral.amazon.com and choose a selling plan.

Have these in front of you:

- Legal name, address, phone, and the LLC name if the account is the LLC
- EIN (or SSN if you sell as a sole prop; the LLC path is the one this manual uses)
- Bank routing and account numbers
- A credit or debit card for the subscription and for any charge Amazon makes
- A government photo ID
- Two-step verification on the phone

Start on the **Individual** plan if the category is open to it and you will not advertise this month. Move to **Professional** ($39.99/month, **FACT**) the month you advertise or cross about 40 sales. If Seller Central says the category requires Professional (automotive parts are a common case), do not list there on Individual. Prefer home and kitchen replacement parts over automotive for the first SKU so you are not learning gating and fitment at the same time.

GTIN: do not buy UPCs this week. The exemption workflow is in Part H.

Trademark: do not file this week. Search the name you want at https://tmsearch.uspto.gov so you do not print a name someone already owns. Filing waits for the scale trigger above.

# Part B — The recipe from a standing start

Follow the steps in order. Each step has a stop line. If you hit the stop line, you do not continue to the next step for that idea.

## Step 1 — Open the records, not the seller account

1. Copy `templates/opportunity-sheet.csv` into a Google Sheet. Name it `Opportunity sheet`.
2. Bookmark amazon.com, ebay.com (Sold filter), trends.google.com, patents.google.com, and reddit.com.
3. Do not open a seller account today.

**Done when:** the sheet exists and the bookmarks exist.

## Step 2 — Build the first 30 rows

Use Part D. Spend the first research block adding 30 rows. Most rows will die. That is the point of the sheet.

Columns you must fill before a row can be scored: product, customer problem, search phrase, amazon competitors (count of relevant listings in the first page), average price of those listings, review count of the top listing, star rating, bad-review themes, material you would use, estimated grams, print time, and the three risk fields. Leave profit blank until Step 5.

**Done when:** 30 rows exist. Volume cells say "not verified" unless you saw a number.

## Step 3 — Search Amazon in a way you can repeat

For each row that still looks alive:

1. Open amazon.com. Paste the search phrase in quotes if it is a part number, otherwise as words. Example: `dryer knob` and then `WPW10111001` if a part number appeared.
2. Set the department only if the results are polluted by books or clothes.
3. Open 10 listings. In a tab called `Amazon log`, record title, price, stars, review count, pack quantity, and "OEM or aftermarket."
4. On the listing with the most reviews, open reviews, sort by recent, read 20. Write the repeated complaint in five words. "Broke in a month" is useful. "Fast shipping" is not.
5. If a molded pack already sells under $12 and your only idea is "the same clip, but printed," mark the row KILL and write the price you saw.

**Stop line:** you invented a monthly sales number. Delete it.

## Step 4 — Find the complaint in the buyer's words

For the ten rows that were not killed:

1. eBay → search the phrase → filter **Sold items** → record 20 rows or write "fewer than 10 sold shown."
2. Google: `"can't find" dryer knob`, `discontinued dryer knob`, `dryer knob replacement`.
3. Google Trends: the head term, United States, past 12 months. Note seasonal or flat.
4. Reddit search: the phrase plus `replacement`. Read the top five threads from the past three years.
5. One repair community: appliancepartspros.com or a brand's parts site, only to see whether the OEM part is still sold and at what list price. Record the list price. That is a FACT about the asking price, not about sales.

**Done when:** each surviving row has a source link.

## Step 5 — Score it

Score each surviving row with this formula. Maximum 100.

| Block | Points | How to assign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand evidence | 0–25 | 0 = no listings and no threads. 10 = threads only. 15 = Amazon listings exist and eBay shows some sold items, price under $18. 20 = sold items and Amazon listings at $18+. 25 = you have your own sales. Never award points for a tool's sales estimate. |
| Economics | 0–25 | Run the calculator. 0 = negative cash contribution. 10 = positive cash, under $15 per printer hour. 18 = $15–$25 per hour. 25 = $25+ per hour and positive after labor. |
| IP and safety | 0–20 | 0 = safety-critical, food contact, children's, or a branded clone. 10 = compatibility claim, patent search not finished. 20 = patent search written, generic function, failure is inconvenience. |
| Manufacturability | 0–15 | 15 = under 40 g, under 45 min, PETG, no support, clearance looser than 0.4 mm. Subtract 5 for ASA, subtract 5 for a snap or a spline, subtract 5 for supports. Floor at 0. |
| Return risk | 0–15 | 15 = one or two dimensions you can measure and show. 8 = a model family with a donor in hand. 0 = "fits most models" with no donor. |

**Hard kills, regardless of score:** economics block under 10, IP and safety block under 10, print over 90 minutes, price under $18, no way to obtain a donor or a standard to measure.

**Done when:** the top three rows are marked TEST and the rest are HOLD or KILL.

## Step 6 — Check intellectual property

For each TEST row:

1. patents.google.com. Search the generic part name. Search again with the OEM assignee if there is one.
2. Open anything filed in the last 20 years that looks like the same shape. Read the claims, not the abstract. If the patent is active and the claims describe your shape, KILL or redesign.
3. tmsearch.uspto.gov. Search the brand name you want to print on the bag. If it is taken in a related class, pick another name.
4. Write five lines in the sheet: patents opened, status, decision, brand search, decision.

**Stop line:** "It's probably expired." Either you read the status or you do not launch.

## Step 7 — Write the CAD brief

Copy the brief template in Part F. Fill every blank from caliper measurements of the donor. If the donor has not arrived, stop.

## Step 8 — Print version 1

PLA is allowed for this print only, as a fit check, if the geometry is not a flexure that depends on PETG's give. Label the part FIT CHECK NOT FOR SALE in marker. Slice at 0.20 mm, 3 walls, 15% infill.

**Done when:** the part is in your hand and labeled.

## Step 9 — Test the fit

Checklist:

- [ ] Mating feature engages without tools, or with the same force as the original
- [ ] It does not fall off when shaken the way the machine shakes
- [ ] Measured dimensions are written next to the CAD dimensions; error over 0.3 mm on a mating feature gets a new revision
- [ ] No crack at a corner when you install and remove it three times
- [ ] You photographed the part on the donor
- [ ] You wrote what the part must not be used for

Three failed fit prints: stop and hire a designer with the brief. Do not start a fourth improvisation.

## Step 10 — Real unit economics

Weigh the part. Use the slicer's grams if the scale agrees within 10%. Time the print. Get a real postage quote (Pirate Ship, eBay labels, or Amazon Buy Shipping) for the packed mailer to a far state and to a near state. Average them. Put that number in the calculator in place of $4.25.

**Stop line:** cash profit per printer hour under $15, or contribution after labor negative. KILL. Do not list it.

## Step 11 — Package

Poly bag, then a padded mailer. A 50 × 70 mm sticker or a printed slip inside the bag with: your brand, the part name, the models you verified, "Not an OEM part," the material, and the ban sentence (example: "Not for gas valves or any control that operates a flame"). No loose plastic bag with an opening of 5 inches or more without a suffocation warning if you later send it to FBA. For the first FBM mailers, a small bag under that size avoids the issue. Measure the packed mailer. If the shortest side is over 0.75 inches, your FBA model must use the large-standard fee, not the small-standard fee.

## Step 12 — List it

eBay first, Amazon FBM the same day if the seller account is open. If the account is not open, eBay alone is a valid test. Part H is the Amazon procedure. Price from the calculator, not from the cheapest competitor. If the only way to match the cheapest competitor is to fail the hour gate, do not match.

Quantity: print nothing to inventory yet. Turn on print-to-order. Your handling time is the print time plus one day, rounded up. A 30-minute knob is a 2-business-day handling time until you have a batch of 10.

## Step 13 — First inventory

After the second order in a 7-day period, print a batch of 10. Not 50. Store them in a closed bin, dry, away from sun. Each part gets the QC checklist.

## Step 14 — FBA, later

Do not create a shipment yet. The trigger is 20 sales and a return rate at or under 8%. Then Part H, FBA shipment. Send 30 days of cover at your actual daily sales rate, not a guess. If you have 20 sales over 40 days, that is 0.5 units a day, so 15 units is 30 days. Send 15, not 200.

## Step 15 — PPC, later

Trigger: Professional plan, 10 sales, return rate at or under 8%, listing photos include the fit photo and the ban sentence.

1. One automatic Sponsored Products campaign, $10 per day, 14 days.
2. One manual exact campaign with the part number and two or three phrases buyers used, $10 per day.
3. Download the search-term report on day 7 and day 14. Negate terms with 2 or more clicks and zero orders if they are the wrong product. Add exact terms that produced an order.

Maximum CPC = your measured conversion rate × pre-ad cash contribution. Until you have 30 clicks of your own, bid half of (0.08 × pre-ad cash contribution) and label 8% as an assumption.

## Step 16 — Measure every Friday

From your sheet, not from memory: units, revenue, fees, postage, refunds, grams, print hours, cash contribution, returns as a percent of units, ad spend, TACOS (ad spend / total revenue).

## Step 17 — Decide

| Decision | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| KILL | Return rate over 15%, or 3 fit complaints in 14 days, or cash profit per printer hour under $15 for two consecutive weeks of real orders, or an IP complaint you cannot cure by editing the listing |
| IMPROVE | Sales exist, returns are 8–15% or fit notes repeat. Pause ads. Change the revision. Do not reorder the old revision. |
| SCALE | 20 sales, returns at or under 8%, hour gate still met on actual grams and actual fees. Then: batch of 10, consider FBA with 30 days of cover, consider ads if not already on, do not add a second SKU until this one has 30 sales unless this one is supply-constrained by a donor-measurement backlog |

## After the first SKU is alive

Keep the weekly research recipe. A second SKU leaves the sheet only when SKU one has 30 sales or has been killed, and the new row passes every gate. Printer two waits for the utilization rule. Molding waits for 100 units a month for 60 days.

The week-by-week calendar is Part M. The one-page version is the Founder's Playbook.
