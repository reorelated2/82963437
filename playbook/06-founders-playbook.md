# The Founder's Playbook

One company. One printer. One measured part. Everything else waits for a number.

The long version is in the other files in this folder. This is the list you follow when you do not want to reread them.

## Today

1. Copy `templates/opportunity-sheet.csv` into a Google Sheet.
2. Order digital calipers, about $20–$35. Do not order a printer.
3. Open eBay, filter Sold, search `dryer knob`. Write down 10 ended prices and whether the photo shows the shaft. If you cannot see prices, write "thin evidence." Do not invent a monthly sales number.
4. Open the Whirlpool-style lesson so it sticks: a genuine tine clip was on Amazon at $6.52 with 23 reviews. A printed single clip at that price fails the hour gate. Kill that shape of idea when you see it.
5. Bookmark amazon.com, ebay.com, trends.google.com, patents.google.com, sellercentral.amazon.com (do not register yet).

**Today is done when** the sheet exists, the calipers are ordered, and ten sold rows are written down.

## This week

1. Add rows until you have 30, using the search phrases in Part D. Mark safety and branded-clone ideas KILL as soon as you see them.
2. For knobs, finish 20 sold rows. Cross out anything under $18.
3. Pick **MODEL YOU SELECT**: the model text that shows up most often above $18 with a shaft photo. Open the OEM parts page. If the OEM part is in stock for about $6, kill the model and take the next one.
4. Search patents.google.com for `dryer knob` and the OEM name. Write what you opened.
5. Order that donor knob, about $10–$25.
6. Search your intended brand at tmsearch.uspto.gov. If the name is taken, pick another. Do not file a trademark.
7. Do not open Amazon Seller Central yet. Do not buy filament in five colors.

**This week is done when** one model is either killed in writing or waiting on a donor you paid for.

## First product

The non-gas cycle knob in Part J.

1. Measure the donor three times. Sketch it.
2. Model v01. Emboss your brand and the version. No OEM logo.
3. Print one in PETG. Time it. Weigh it.
4. Fit it. Three install-and-remove cycles. Three failed versions, then a paid designer with the brief in Part F.
5. Get a real postage quote. Run `python3 playbook/tools/unit_economics.py`. The planning model at 22 g, 30 minutes, $27.99, FBM, $4.25 postage, 10% returns, and no ads is about **$17.86 cash contribution and $35.73 per printer hour**. Your postage number replaces $4.25 before you trust that.
6. Kill it if cash profit per printer hour is under $15 or if contribution after labor at $20/hour is negative.
7. Package with the slip that says what the shaft measures and that it is not for a gas valve.
8. List on eBay. List on Amazon FBM once the account and the GTIN exemption photos are done. Handling time 2 business days. On-hand inventory: zero. Print to order.

## First sale

1. Print the current version only. QC it against the donor shaft.
2. Ship in the mailer you quoted. Record postage, fees, grams, and minutes in the sheet the same day.
3. Do not buy a second spool "just in case" unless the first spool will run out inside the next ten orders.
4. Do not add a second model to the listing.
5. If the buyer says it does not fit, pay the return. Do not argue them into forcing a spline onto a D-shaft. That return is the data.

## First 100 sales

This may take many months. The gates are the gates at sale 10, 20, 30, and 100. "First 100" is not a permission slip to skip them.

- At 10 sales, returns at or under 8%: Professional plan and the two $10/day campaigns are allowed.
- At 20 sales, same return rule: FBA is allowed, 30 days of cover, placement fee written down first.
- At 30 sales: a second SKU may enter CAD if it passes the paper gates. Not before.
- At 100 in a single month: write the process, request three mold quotes, still no tool deposit. Trademark only if the brand is already on 50 packages ($350 USPTO base fee per class, plus whatever an attorney charges if you hire one).
- Printer two only if sold work has exceeded the first printer for two straight weeks and the hour rate is still at least $25.
- If returns cross 15% at any point, you are no longer on this rung. You are on the kill rule.

## $5,000 a month

Revenue, not profit. You are here only if the orders are yours.

1. Read contribution, not revenue. If cash contribution is under about 30% of revenue after ads and postage, fix price, returns, or ads before you add a SKU.
2. The Friday dashboard is mandatory. Days of cover between 14 and 45.
3. A freelance CAD designer is allowed for the next shaft. A print helper is allowed only with the QC checklist in their hand.
4. Keep paid research tools optional. Your order report outranks them.
5. Do not move into a rented workshop because the printer is loud. Fix the enclosure and the schedule first.

## $10,000 a month

1. Two printers of the same model are justified only if both would be busy on gate-passing SKUs. Otherwise raise price.
2. FBA only for SKUs whose return rate you already trust. New variants stay FBM.
3. A bookkeeper monthly. You keep the kill decision.
4. Mold quotes get the crossover formula. A 5,000-unit payback at 100 units a month is a "no."
5. Brand Registry if the trademark is filed or registered. Use it to protect your listing content, not as a reason to launch ten products.

## $25,000 a month

This is the excellent shape sustained: several fast, high-price parts, more than one printer, returns still under 8%, hour rate still above $25 after ads. If you hit the revenue number with hour rates near $15, you bought yourself a job. Cut SKUs until the hour rate recovers.

1. A real molder gets a deposit only when the crossover sits inside demand you have already seen for 60 days, with two quotes.
2. Hire a person to QC and ship. You still approve new SKUs, safety calls, and ad caps.
3. Insurance limits get a fresh conversation with the broker now that revenue is real.
4. The research Tuesday continues. The catalog does not jump to 30 SKUs because revenue did. Every new SKU still passes the same gate as the first knob.

## The line you do not cross

You do not sell a part that can injure someone because the printer is paid for. You do not invent a sales number because a row looks empty. You do not send a new version to FBA to find out if it fits. The donor shaft in the QC bin is the product.
